A Dynamic Pricing Strategy Benchmarked Against Your Comp Set

Before pricing starts, you review the competitive set we'll benchmark your dynamic pricing strategy against: 5 to 8 comparable properties. Once you've agreed it, that comparison guides our ongoing rate checks and keeps your strategy grounded in your market.

How we build and maintain your competitive set.

  1. Map comparable properties

    We pick 5 to 8 properties with a similar location, property type, size and price level.

  2. Review list with you

    You check the list before pricing starts and can add or remove any hotel at any time.

  3. Check rates daily

    We check your rates against the set every day.

  4. Update every quarter

    We update the competitive set every quarter.

Who RateHarbor Is and How We Forecast Demand

We build your demand calendar 12 months ahead from at least 12 months of your booking history, or 24 months if you have it.

We combine your booking patterns with market evidence to classify demand, and we revise the calendar as new signals appear. Some events are announced late and others never appear on public calendars, so the calendar is a working document rather than a fixed forecast.

If your booking history is short or patchy, your initial calendar is less reliable and leans more heavily on market data. As your outsourced hotel revenue and OTA management company, RateHarbor assigns you one dedicated specialist from our 12-person team.

Your specialist has at least 5 years of experience with independent and boutique hotels. Each specialist handles no more than 10 properties, and the team covers you all 365 days of the year.

How we build and update your demand calendar.

  1. Gather booking and market data

    Booking history, pace and pickup, daily competitor rates and live market data from your pricing tools and OTA extranets.

  2. Find local events

    City event calendars, convention centre schedules, public and school holidays and sudden rate jumps across your competitive set.

  3. Rate each date

    Each date is rated low, normal, high or peak demand by its expected effect on occupancy and competitor rates.

  4. Re-rate as signals appear

    Dates are re-rated as soon as competitor rates or booking pace show an event not yet logged.

Tracking Results Against Your Competitive Set

We track three indexes against your competitive set: occupancy (MPI), ADR (ARI) and RevPAR (RGI).

You see them in weekly and monthly reports and in an in-depth quarterly review. A score of 100 means you're matching your competitive set, and a higher score means you're gaining share. We judge pricing mainly by how those indexes move, not by occupancy or ADR alone.

We compare your results with the same period last year and with your budget, which separates pricing performance from seasonality and wider market movement. That's why we review your competitive set with you and update it every quarter.

What Every Hotel Pricing Setup Includes

Room-type foundations

Every room type gets its own base rate and floor, set against where your property stands within the competitive set.

Cost-based floors

We calculate floors before adding any rules. They cover housekeeping, laundry, amenities, utilities and the minimum margin you choose. If floors are set only against competitors, bookings from expensive channels are exposed to returns below what they cost to service.

Channel-safe returns

We gross up floors for your highest channel commission and payment fees, so your minimum net return is protected on every channel.

On very weak nights, these floors keep you priced above distressed competitors, so you never sell a room below what it costs to service.

Demand-responsive rates

Each room type's rate levels move with that room type's own occupancy and booking pace. We don't treat all categories alike.

Protected category gaps

We link higher categories to your reference room by a fixed amount or percentage and keep at least the minimum gap you approve.

Categories that fill faster climb further, but an upgrade never prices at or below the category beneath it.

Booking controls

Your pricing setup includes lead-time and last-minute rules, minimum and maximum stays, and closed-to-arrival and closed-to-departure controls.

Longer stays and packages

Pricing for longer stays and packages is a core part of the strategy we deliver to you.

Separate channel markups

We set separate markups for OTAs, GDS and your direct channel instead of using one markup across all channels.

Your Room-Type Setup, Built Live in PriceLabs

If your hotel runs PriceLabs, our revenue manager builds your strategy live over Zoom, working alongside the staff who set your rates.

  1. 01

    Provide named access

    Set up a named PriceLabs login for our specialist instead of sharing a password. Give it only the permissions the work needs.

  2. 02

    Prepare your records

    Have your room-type list ready, along with at least 12 months of booking history, or 24 months if you have it.

  3. 03

    Reserve staff time

    Your day-to-day rate setter and key staff join for the whole call and make the decisions with us. That way they understand the settings and don't undo or misuse them afterward.

  4. 04

    Build every room type

    We set up the agreed settings for each room type inside PriceLabs, including BAR levels and seasonal and event adjustments. Your team stays involved the whole way.

  5. 05

    Complete one live call

    Set aside about 90 minutes for 1 to 2 room types, rising to 3 hours for 7 to 10 room types. Your staff need to be available for the entire call.

  6. 06

    Receive your written strategy

    We walk you through the finished setup and write down why we chose each setting. The PriceLabs work, including your written handover, takes 1 to 2 business days.

  • Self-run after setup

    What we handle
    We recalibrate every 6 to 8 weeks, or sooner when demand shifts, to correct drift.
    What your team handles
    You review the tool's recommendations and competitive rates every day. You also add events and adjust seasonal rules, minimum stays and floors as demand changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you build a pricing strategy for a newly opened property with little booking history, and what evidence would you use instead?

Yes. Instead of past bookings, we start with competitive-set rates, market demand data and your budget. As your bookings build up, we move over to your own pace and pickup. We refine the strategy at revisits every 6 to 8 weeks.

My hotel doesn't use PriceLabs. Can you still build the strategy live in my own system, or do I need to set up a pricing tool first?

Yes. We build live in the pricing tool or RMS you already use, such as Beyond Pricing. We can also build in your PMS or channel-manager rate settings, such as Cloudbeds or SiteMinder. The session runs 90 minutes to 3 hours. It covers floors, base rates, BAR tiers, seasonal and event changes, stay restrictions, length-of-stay rules and channel markups. You don't need a replacement system or a dynamic pricing tool. If you want a tool, we recommend one that fits your property, and we take no vendor referral commissions. We set it up before the session.

Can you keep our negotiated corporate, group and wholesale rates outside dynamic adjustments while pricing publicly available rooms dynamically?

Yes. We load your negotiated rates as fixed plans that aren't linked to BAR. We keep those rates and their allotments out of dynamic adjustments and change them only with your written approval. Your public rooms stay dynamically priced. If a contracted rate is more than 15% below BAR on a high-demand date, we flag it for your next contract-renewal review.

If our team manually changes a rate or restriction, how do you prevent the managed pricing process from overwriting that change unintentionally?

We never overwrite your manual rate or restriction changes. Our daily review flags and logs each override. We then lock that date and room type in your pricing tool, so the automation skips it. We discuss it with you on your next strategy call, and it stays in place until you approve removing it.

How do you prevent or reverse inappropriate rate changes when booking data becomes stale or a pricing integration fails?

If booking data goes more than 12 hours without refreshing, we hold rates at their last verified levels and apply your floors. A sync or integration failure triggers an alert, and we suspend automated updates. We protect the affected OTA availability while we fix the fault within 4 hours, and we let you know the same day. We restore any mistaken rates from the change log that same day. Within 24 hours after syncing resumes, we reconcile reservations against your PMS. A second specialist reviews every automated rule change before it goes live. Your reservations team loads any bookings we flag as missing and tells us about any changes they made directly during the outage.

What happens when a rate sync or pricing integration breaks.

  1. Alert and suspend updates

    We get an alert and suspend automated updates.

  2. Protect OTA availability

    We set stop-sells or lower availability straight in the affected OTA extranets so no channel can oversell.

  3. Resolve within 4 hours

    We resolve the fault within 4 hours and let you know that same day.

  4. Revert mistaken rates

    Any rate pushed by mistake goes back to its prior setting from the change log on the same day.

  5. Reconcile reservations

    Once syncing works again, we match every reservation received during the outage with your PMS records within 24 hours.
