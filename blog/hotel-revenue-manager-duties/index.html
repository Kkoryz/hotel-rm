What does a revenue manager do for a hotel?

By Ethan DelgadoPublished 17 November 20267 min read

A hotel revenue manager forecasts demand, sets room rates and availability, and manages booking channels to improve financial performance. They coordinate with hotel teams and review results so the owner can understand what changed, why it changed and whether it helped. Cvent's hotel revenue manager guide explains these responsibilities.

At RateHarbor, we provide this work remotely in your hotel's own systems. For an owner, the useful output is a documented decision, clear authority to implement it and a review that accounts for costs.

A hotel revenue manager's responsibilities, from pricing to results

Your revenue manager brings pricing, forecasting, competitor analysis and distribution into the decisions your hotel team makes. Occupancy is the share of available rooms sold; average daily rate, or ADR, is room revenue divided by rooms sold; revenue per available room, or RevPAR, spreads room revenue across all available rooms. Cvent's performance metrics explain how these measures work together.

Follow a decision from booking signal to owner review

At an independent hotel in Ohio, our remote revenue specialist reviewed an upcoming festival weekend. The pickup export showed new reservations since Monday, but the transient segment also included a tentative group block. Reservations corrected the block status in the property management system, then our specialist refreshed the forecast and reconciled room-type availability with the channel manager. The decision record kept the original export, corrected segment totals and matching availability checks.

Competitor rates were rising, but the corrected pickup supported a smaller increase than the pricing tool suggested. We considered holding rates, raising the flexible rate or adding a minimum stay. We chose the rate increase without a stay restriction because demand for adjacent nights remained weak. The GM agreed to the event strategy. Our specialist then published it and checked the same room type, dates, occupancy and cancellation terms on the direct booking engine, Booking.com and Expedia.

At the next weekly review, our owner report showed the rate decision alongside pickup, cancellations, achieved rates and channel costs. The revised rate was still bookable across the affected channels, but the stay dates had not yet passed. We left the restriction unchanged and scheduled another review before arrival. Final contribution and the effect of festival demand were still unresolved.

Consider demand signals and channel choices together: a competitor's advertised rate alone does not tell you what your guests will pay. Read the owner's scorecard beyond gross revenue

Our hypothetical owner scorecard uses assumed USD inputs to compare equivalent 10-night periods at a 50-room hotel, with 500 available room nights in each period. The competitive set and room availability remain unchanged. Budget RevPAR for the current period is $130. Acquisition costs include commissions, payment fees and allocated booking spend. Variable operating cost is $30 per occupied room night for housekeeping, laundry and amenities.

MeasureEarlier periodCurrent period
Rooms sold / occupancy350 / 70%375 / 75%
ADR$180$184
Gross room revenue$63,000$69,000
Hotel / market RevPAR$126 / $120$138 / $132
Market-relative index105104.55
Acquisition costs / net room revenue$6,300 / $56,700$8,280 / $60,720
Variable operating costs / contribution$10,500 / $46,200$11,250 / $49,470

Calculate gross revenue as rooms sold × ADR, and RevPAR as gross revenue ÷ available room nights. The market-relative index is hotel RevPAR ÷ market RevPAR × 100: $126 ÷ $120 × 100 = 105, compared with $138 ÷ $132 × 100 = 104.55, rounded. Hotel RevPAR rose about 9.52%, while market RevPAR rose 10%. Current RevPAR beat the $130 budget by $8, but performance relative to the market slipped. Net revenue is gross revenue minus acquisition costs. To calculate contribution, then subtract the specified variable costs: $60,720 − $11,250 = $49,470.

Lighthouse's benchmarking guidance explains market-relative performance. If rooms are taken out of service or the competitive set changes, reconcile those differences before you interpret the index.

This contribution excludes fixed payroll, rent, debt service and other overhead, so it is not full hotel profit. Read it alongside the diary's pricing action and final stay-date results. A before-and-after comparison cannot separate the manager's effect from an event, changing demand or a different guest mix.

What skills does a hotel revenue manager need?

Your hotel revenue manager needs analytical, forecasting, pricing, distribution and financial skills, along with the ability to use hotel systems and explain decisions clearly. How much depth you need depends on your property's complexity and the support available.

These skills feed directly into the work above: commercial judgment guides pricing, systems knowledge helps ensure decisions are carried out correctly, and communication helps everyone agree on a decision. See Cvent's role guide, Revenue Hub's revenue manager traits and EHL's revenue management guide for commercial skills, technology and collaboration, and pricing and data quality.

Look for observable competence

For a US independent hotel, we recommend putting safe decisions ahead of advanced modeling. Treat this as a practical hiring framework, not a universal credential requirement. If your property has complex group business, many room types or limited staff coverage, you need deeper expertise at entry.

Use a complete work sample

Try this invented teaching exercise for a single future night. Assume you have 50 sellable rooms, 30 bookings on the books at $180, and 10 new bookings during the past 7 days, with 2 cancellations already reflected in the on-books total. Assume another 10 bookings and 2 cancellations before arrival. Comparable competitor rates are $190–$210. Assume half of all sold room nights are direct at a 4% acquisition cost and half are OTA at 16%, with the same ADR on both channels. Variable room cost is $30. Forecast occupancy, recommend an action and identify what information is missing.

Here is the answer key: 30 + 10 − 2 = 38 occupied rooms, or 76% occupancy. With ADR unchanged at $180, gross revenue is $6,840. The weighted acquisition rate is 10%, giving acquisition costs of $684 and net revenue of $6,156. Subtract 38 × $30 = $1,140 in variable costs to get $5,016 contribution. This assumes the remaining cancellations release inventory for resale and there are no additional no-shows.

Holding $180 is defensible if demand is fragile. You could instead test $190 for the expected 10 new bookings while the surviving 28 existing bookings stay at $180. Gross revenue then becomes $6,940, acquisition costs $694 and contribution $5,106, assuming volume and channel mix hold. You need to test that assumption. Before recommending a stay restriction, ask for historical pickup at the same lead time, event certainty, room-type availability, cancellation terms and competitor comparability.

Rate each response as strong, partial or missing for data validation, commercial reasoning, uncertainty, implementation checks and clarity. Strong answers reconcile booking status, show the contribution arithmetic, explain competing options, identify assumptions, name approval and publication checks, and state what will trigger a review. Do not let unsupported confidence about the best rate outweigh sound reasoning.

Test diagnosis and communication

For a troubleshooting role-play, imagine the pricing tool recommends a sharp increase after treating a tentative group block as confirmed demand. Inspect the block status, segment mapping and feed timestamp, then correct the classification with reservations and refresh the forecast. Preserve any owner-set override, seek approval for a major strategy change, and verify the resulting rate and availability guests see. Attach before-and-after input captures to the decision record and escalate a stale feed to the system provider.

In the same team role-play, sales wants to discount immediately to secure occupancy, while the revenue manager wants to protect rate while checking whether the group will confirm. The recommendation is to hold the flexible rate, ask sales for a firm block deadline and keep unrestricted inventory bookable. The GM authorizes that approach, the revenue manager puts it into effect, and reservations checks availability. Review the decision when the block deadline passes.

The written decision should let you, as the owner, see the tradeoff, who is responsible and what triggers the next review without having to reconstruct the conversation.

Make revenue management responsibilities clear at your hotel

Make the role accountable with a documented commercial decision, an agreed responsibility map and an owner review that takes market movement and costs into account.
